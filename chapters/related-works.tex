GeoMedia positions itself among existing online social media platforms, offering core functionality for sharing location-based content anchored to geographic coordinates. Content is visible only within a user-defined distance, or it can be used to create various real-world scenarios, such as scavenger hunts or city tour guides.
The application enables users to explore the real world through enriched map-based content contributed by other users, who can upload pictures or other multimedia files associated with specific locations.

Several other social media platforms utilize a mobile device’s GPS module to enhance user experience or provide additional functionality. However, none fully implement the concept behind GeoMedia or its versatility in creating varios application scenario and explore content uploaded.

\section{Foursquare Swarm}

Foursquare Swarm is the spin-off of a formely app `Foursquare City Guide', which once provided personalized recoomandations of places near a user's current location based on it interests or previous browsing history and check-in (place where the user has been).
Foursquare has been discontinued in 2024 and it functionalities has been integrated into Swarm, another app that also allows users to share their position with realatives. The apps also create a personal lifelog of places visited thus to share it with others. \\

Any user for each place visited, can leave a review or uploads media, but this is associated to a physical place like a store, coffee, restaurant or something, cannot be done in desired coordination and do not provide any kind of post confining, every users can access to it. 
The application also incentive the usage by making the user earning badges by visiting places and uploading content to it. These functionalities and mechanism are quite the same of also other solutions like Google Maps (see Section~\ref{sec:google-maps}).

\subsection{Dodgeball and Google Latitude}
Dodgeball later acquired by Google and rebranded as `Google Latitude' \cite{site:google-latitude} was quite the precursor of Foursquare, simply allowing to share current locations with friends through a map.
Google Latitude was discontinued in 2013, integrated into another discontinued social media by Google (Google+ \cite{site:google-plus}) and finally migrated into Google Maps in 2018.

\section{Snapchat}

This brings user to check it daily in order to not missing out anything and to keep up with realtives life. The app also consent to see firends current location on the app, and geographic group closer content into a single flow of information.
