\section{Overview}



Over the last two decades, mobile devices such as smartphones and tablets have become more and more present in everyday life, shaping people`s habits and changing the way they interact with technology and digital applications, almost replacing traditional desktop software and web browsing.

There are many examples of this trend in modern society. For instance, when planning a trip or driving, smartphones assist users through apps like Google Maps or Waze. These apps don`t just provide directions—they also improve the experience with real-time traffic updates, road event notifications, and information about available parking. In this way, the surrounding environment is enriched with digital details. Another example is Google Lens, which can identify objects from a photograph, or Shazam that can recognize a song through the device`s microphone.

The technology integration has become an important key even in workplaces and public spaces, from the resturant that provide a digital menu by a tablet, to customers checking a shopping lists. Similary, many museums allowing visitors to scan QR codes to access audio-guided tours or unlock Augmented Reality functionalities that enhance the experience. 


The wider adoption of smartphones has been driven by two main factors:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hardware}: the increase in computational performance, battery life, fast connectivity, and the integration of several built-in sensors such as gyroscopes, GPS, NFC, light sensors, cameras, and vibration motors. These enable applications to interact with different fields such as health, education, and, through motion-sensors or cameras, a coexistence with physical world, creating a more immersive and user experience.

    \item \textbf{Software}: the development and presence of mobile applications has played a crucial role. In fact, platforms such as `Windows Phone' were eventually abandoned due to the lack of applications compared to operating system like `iOS' and `Android'. This market higly influenced application design, pushing developers towards a `mobile-first' approach and encouraging the exploitation of device sensors to create a more unique and sophisitcated applications.
\end{enumerate}


\section{Social networks}


Alongside the adoption of mobile technologies, social networks have emerged as a main role in the technology market, placing their position among the most used applications globally.
These platforms have totally changed the way people communicate digitally, by providing real-time messaging capabilities in both one-to-one and one-to-many formats, while also allowing users to share any kind of multimedia, including photos, videos, audio recordings, and other files. 
Applications such as WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram, and Facebook have attracted billions of users worldwide, becoming deeply embedded in daily communication practices across diverse social, professional, and public contexts.

These applications allow users to share diverse types of content—including photos, audio recordings, videos, and text messages—with individuals, groups, or even a global audience. This has expanded the possibilities for communication, collaboration, and the dissemination of information across personal, professional, and public contexts.




By facilitating the instantaneous exchange of information and supporting multiple forms of interaction, social networks have not only expanded the scope of personal and professional communication but have also transformed the ways in which knowledge, ideas, and content are disseminated and consumed. These platforms are predominantly user-centric, leveraging personalized recommendation algorithms that tailor content according to users’ interests, interactions, and browsing behavior. As a result, they foster engagement while facilitating the creation and maintenance of digital relationships across diverse communities. By bridging geographical distances and enabling individuals to connect around shared interests, social networks have contributed to the development of increasingly interconnected digital ecosystems. Beyond their communication functions, these platforms have evolved into important channels for entertainment, news consumption, content creation, and professional networking, reflecting their multifaceted role in contemporary society. Consequently, social media has redefined the landscape of digital connectivity, creating new opportunities for collaboration, engagement, and participation on a global scale.
